A free and diverse media landscape is widely considered essential to healthy democracy. Yet we know surprisingly little about how ideologically diverse national media markets actually are, or how that diversity changes when governments interfere with press freedom. The main obstacle has been practical: collecting content from hundreds of news outlets across countries and years is technically demanding, and existing datasets simply do not exist. This project provides a proof-of-concept for solving that problem at scale.

The central methodological contribution is demonstrating that CommonCrawl, a freely available web archive that has systematically indexed billions of web pages since 2008, and is widely used in the training of large language models, can serve as a powerful new data source for media research. Despite its enormous potential for the social sciences, it has never been used in political science or communication research. By developing a reproducible pipeline to extract, process, and analyze news content from this archive, the project opens the door to comparative media research at a scale and historical depth that was previously out of reach.
